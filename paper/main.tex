\begin{abstract}
We determine the diameter of the full labelled, undirected bistellar flip graph
of the ten integer moment-curve points in dimension four. The graph has 4,824
vertices and 15,120 edges, is connected, and has diameter 21 and radius 15.
Exactly seven unordered pairs attain the diameter; they form two orbits of
sizes two and five under the dihedral action on the vertex labels. We give a
computer-assisted proof based on exact complete enumeration, all-source
breadth-first search, and a checkable 21-flip path with a matching lower-bound
potential. A standalone verifier reconstructs the geometry, census, graph and
distances without importing the main implementation. Executed comparisons with
TOPCOM and previously published graph-generation code give identical objects.
A Lean 4 proof additionally certifies diameter 21 for the explicit finite graph. Its identification with the geometric flip graph is justified by the exact enumeration arguments and independent data checks. The accompanying source and data allow the certificates to be reproduced.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} cyclic polytope; triangulation; bistellar flip;
graph diameter; exact enumeration; computational certificate.

\section{Introduction and Related Work}
The flip distance between two triangulations is the least number of bistellar
flips transforming one into the other. Determining the largest such distance
is a natural finite question even for a small cyclic polytope. We study the
complete graph for ten points on the integer moment curve in dimension four,
retaining labels and including nonregular triangulations.

The triangulation count is already known: Rambau's enumeration tables list
357 triangulations for $C(9,4)$ and 4,824 for $C(10,4)$
\cite[Table 7]{rambau2026}; see also the enumeration work of Joswig and
Kastner \cite{joswig}. Connectivity is also known from the theory of
triangulations of cyclic polytopes \cite{rambau1997}. The survey of Rambau and
Reiner \cite[Theorem 6.5 and Corollary 6.9]{survey} gives connectivity and
bounds that specialize to $15\leq\diam G\leq30$ in this case.

Our contribution is an explicit, reproducible determination of the exact
value 21, together with the radius, all diametral pairs, their dihedral
orbits, and independently checkable finite certificates. The computation was
motivated by TheoremDB problem P2804 \cite{theoremdb}. A bounded literature
search did not locate a prior statement of this exact diameter; this does not
establish priority. In particular, Williams's publicly available code
\cite{williamscode} has made this graph computable since 2020. We therefore
make no claim to a new enumeration count, a new connectivity theorem, or a
general formula for flip-graph diameters. Williams's ICIAM 2023 talk
\cite{williamsiciam} also explicitly announced discussion of diameters; its
available abstract does not report the value for this instance.

\section{Definitions and Exact Statement}
Let $p_i=(i,i^2,i^3,i^4)$, $1\leq i\leq10$, and
$P=\conv\{p_1,\ldots,p_{10}\}$. A triangulation is a face-to-face covering by
4-simplices with vertices among these points. The simple undirected graph $G$
joins triangulations that differ by one geometric bistellar flip, of cost one.
It includes all labelled triangulations, without regularity restrictions or a
quotient by symmetry. It is not the directed monotone-flip graph.

\begin{theorem}[Exact finite computation]
For the stated coordinates, $G$ has $4824$ vertices and $15120$ edges. It is
connected, with diameter $21$ and radius $15$. There are exactly seven unordered
diametral pairs, in two dihedral orbits of sizes two and five. In the canonical zero-based numbering of the supplied census
they are
\[
\begin{split}
&(529,4174),\ (639,3642),\ (1049,2825),\ (1162,3352),\\
&(1639,2511),\ (1962,4013),\ (2206,4307).
\end{split}
\]
\end{theorem}
The theorem is certified by the executed algorithms and the correctness
arguments below. The primary certificate uses exact integer arithmetic throughout. An optional
SciPy distance comparison uses floating point with integrality checks. All triangulations have 21 maximal
simplices in the enumerated census; that observation is not an enumeration
assumption.

\section{Exact Geometry}
For increasing $a_1,\ldots,a_5$, the homogeneous determinant is
\[
\det(1,a_i,a_i^2,a_i^3,a_i^4)_{i=1}^{5}
=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq5}(a_j-a_i)>0.
\]
Thus any five points are affinely independent. A quartet $F$ supports a hull
facet precisely when $\prod_{v\in F}(t-v)$ has the same sign at all remaining
labels. This exact predicate gives 35 tetrahedral boundary facets. Coning the
facets not containing vertex 1 to vertex 1 gives a pulling triangulation.
The sum of its determinants is $2737152$, so
$\operatorname{vol}_4(P)=2737152/24=114048$.

Every circuit is a six-element set $C=\{s_1<\cdots<s_6\}$. Its affine
dependence has coefficients proportional to
\[
\lambda_j=\frac{1}{\prod_{k\ne j}(s_j-s_k)}.
\]
The identities $\sum_j\lambda_j s_j^r=0$, $0\leq r\leq4$, follow by comparing
the coefficient of degree five in Lagrange interpolation of $t^r$ on six
nodes. The signs alternate, giving three positive and three negative
coefficients. There are $\binom{10}{6}=210$ circuits.

\begin{lemma}[Proper intersection criterion]
Two distinct maximal simplices $A,B$ intersect exactly in their common face
if and only if there is no signed circuit with $C^+\subseteq A$ and
$C^-\subseteq B$, or the reverse containment.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A forbidden signed circuit provides equal convex combinations on its two
parts. The Radon point lies in both simplices and outside their common face:
otherwise uniqueness of barycentric coordinates in an affinely independent
simplex would force zero coefficients on vertices outside that face. Conversely,
an intersection point outside the common face gives two convex representations.
Cancel coefficients on common vertices to get a nonzero affine dependence with
positive support in $A$ and negative support in $B$. The cone of dependencies with these coordinate-wise sign constraints is
pointed and nonzero, so it has a nonzero extreme ray $x$. If the kernel on
$\operatorname{supp}(x)$ had dimension greater than one, an independent kernel
vector $y$ on the same support would make both $x+\varepsilon y$ and
$x-\varepsilon y$ sign-compatible for sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$.
This contradicts extremality. A dependence on a proper subset of that support
would also be independent of $x$, so none exists. Thus $x$ is a signed circuit
with the required containments.
\end{proof}

This criterion verifies the full face-to-face condition, not just disjoint
4-dimensional interiors. For a compatible collection of simplices in $P$,
equality of the sum of their exact volumes with that of $P$ certifies coverage.
Indeed their union is closed; any omitted point would have a relatively open
neighborhood in $P$ of positive 4-volume, contradicting the equality.

\section{Complete Enumeration}
\subsection{Frontier Enumerator}
The main algorithm starts with the 35 boundary tetrahedra as requirements.
Adding a simplex toggles its five tetrahedral facets. For a compatible partial
triangulation the remaining requirements are unfilled boundary facets and
internal facets incident to exactly one selected simplex. Compatibility
prevents three maximal simplices from sharing a tetrahedral facet: two would
lie on the same side and overlap in their interiors.

Select a required facet with the fewest currently available compatible
simplices, with deterministic tie breaking, and branch over all such simplices.
A zero candidate count prunes the branch. Accept a leaf only with no remaining
requirements and exact total volume equal to $\operatorname{vol}_4(P)$.

\begin{lemma}[Completeness and uniqueness]
The frontier algorithm lists every triangulation exactly once.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix any full triangulation $T$ extending a visited partial collection. An
unfilled boundary facet belongs to exactly one simplex in $T$. An exposed
internal facet already has one incident selected simplex and has exactly one
other incident simplex in $T$. Thus whichever required facet the algorithm
selects, exactly one branch is compatible with staying inside $T$. That branch
cannot be pruned: its needed simplex remains available, and disjoint interiors
prevent the partial volume from exceeding the hull volume. Iteration reaches
$T$. Once requirements are empty and volume is full, no proper extension is
possible. Deterministic facet selection makes the branch leading to any fixed
$T$ unique. Soundness follows from proper intersections and exact coverage.
\end{proof}

The runs visit 2,109 search nodes for $C(9,4)$ and 28,774 for $C(10,4)$, finding
357 and 4,824 triangulations, respectively.

\subsection{Independent Enumeration and TOPCOM}
The standalone checker imports no main implementation module. It obtains
volumes, hull-facet signs and circuit coefficients by recursive Laplace
determinants, instead of Vandermonde products, quartic signs and reciprocal
products. It enumerates compatible subsets in increasing simplex-index order,
pruning only if some required facet cannot be filled by any future simplex.
Any full triangulation supplies such a future simplex at every one of its
prefixes, so this pruning is complete. At leaves exact volumes certify coverage.
This separate search visits 248,097 nodes for $C(10,4)$ and obtains exactly the
same ordered simplex lists. For $C(9,4)$ it visits 14,869 nodes and finds 357.

TOPCOM 1.1.2 was actually run in extension mode
\texttt{points2alltriangs --nosymmetries -v}. The input has an empty symmetry
list; no regularity filter is used. Its full outputs agree object for object
with both enumerations after converting zero-based point labels to $1,\ldots,n$.
The raw outputs and diagnostics are included. Matching a published count alone
is not used as a completeness proof.

\section{Construction of the Complete Flip Graph}
For each circuit the two local triangulations are
\[
\mathcal T_+=\{C\setminus\{i\}:i\in C^+\},\qquad
\mathcal T_-=\{C\setminus\{i\}:i\in C^-\}.
\]
Both contain three 4-simplices, tile $\conv C$, and have the same boundary.
For example, shifting any nonnegative affine representation along the circuit
dependence to either endpoint of its feasible interval produces these two
decompositions. For every global triangulation and every circuit, both
directions of replacement are tested. The target must occur in the completely
enumerated, geometrically validated census; otherwise the run fails.

Every accepted pair differs by one genuine local replacement on the same
geometric support. Conversely every flip has one of the 210 circuit supports:
general position excludes smaller circuits, and a full-dimensional circuit
leaves no nonempty join link. Hence the edge list is complete. Symmetry of the
directed tests, absence of loops and duplicate edges, and inverse replacements
are checked. The result has 15,120 distinct undirected edges.

As an additional check, extract each triangulation's 15 internal triangles.
By the one-internal-simplex exchange characterization
\cite[Theorems 1.2--1.3]{williams}, two such encodings are adjacent exactly when
they share 14 triangles. An independent bucket construction gives precisely
the same edge set. This extra characterization is not needed to justify the
circuit replacement algorithm.

\subsection{Previously Published Graph-Generation Code}
As an additional implementation check we executed the function
\texttt{even\_dict\_hst\_1(10,2)} from Williams's \texttt{hst-orders}
repository \cite{williamscode}, pinned to commit\\
{\small\texttt{278dd2ed9192def321d566bc9fd0779a46fc40a4}}.
It constructs the upward-flip graph in an internal-triangle encoding.
Forgetting orientations and converting encodings gives exactly the same
4,824 vertices and 15,120 edges.

Sage was unavailable: six required functions were extracted by Python's AST
from the original file and executed with their bodies unchanged, using NumPy
and \texttt{itertools}; the unused Sage \texttt{Poset} import was omitted.
The original file, its GPL-3.0 license, its hash, and the wrapper are supplied.
This is execution of previously authored functions, rather than a native Sage
run or an external human review. SciPy's all-pairs unweighted shortest-path
routine on the resulting undirected graph reproduced the complete distance
matrix after checking that every entry is an integer between 0 and 21.

\section{Connectivity and Exact Distances}
Queue-based BFS finds one component and computes a full distance row from each
of the 4,824 vertices. A second implementation uses bitset layers, with
$L_{r+1}=(\bigcup_{u\in L_r}N(u))\setminus\bigcup_{j\leq r}L_j$.
Induction proves that these layers consist exactly of vertices at distance $r$.
Every eccentricity and the complete distance-matrix hash agree.

Thus the upper bound $\diam G\leq21$ holds for every pair, not just sampled
sources. For the lower bound, \texttt{data/witness.json} contains an integer
potential $h$ on all vertices with
\[
h(529)=0,\quad h(4174)=21,\quad |h(u)-h(v)|\leq1\quad\text{for every edge }uv.
\]
Every path between these endpoints has length at least 21. The supplied path
\[
\begin{split}
529,&199,194,171,141,4,3,0,1,17,21,\\
&25,26,47,364,1942,2313,2619,2630,3202,3251,4174
\end{split}
\]
has exactly 21 edges. Each is annotated with the six circuit labels and the
three removed and three inserted maximal simplices. This proves equality.
There are $11,633,076$ unordered distinct vertex pairs, exactly seven of which
have distance 21. The label permutations
\[
 i\longmapsto 1+\bigl(\varepsilon(i-1)+k\bmod10\bigr),
 \qquad \varepsilon\in\{-1,1\},\quad k=0,\ldots,9,
\]
induce 20 distinct permutations of the census. We checked that each preserves
the entire edge set and the set of diametral pairs. This is a graph action;
we do not assert that these permutations are Euclidean isometries of the
chosen coordinates. Its orbits on the diametral pairs are
\[
\begin{array}{cl}
2 & (529,4174),\ (1162,3352),\\
5 & (639,3642),\ (1049,2825),\ (1639,2511),\\
  & (1962,4013),\ (2206,4307).
\end{array}
\]
The finite action check is implemented in
\texttt{tools/structural\_summary.py} and recorded in
\texttt{results/structural\_summary.json}.

\section{A Kernel-Checked Graph Certificate}
We additionally formalized the diameter claim for the explicit finite graph
in Lean 4.24.0 \cite{lean4}. The theorem
\texttt{P2804.Fixed.graph\_diameter\_twenty\_one}
proves that every ordered pair has a walk of at most 21 edges, and that some
ordered pair has no walk shorter than 21. It uses only the standard library.
All closed finite checks use \texttt{decide +kernel}; no admitted proof,
custom axiom or native-computation axiom is used. The final axiom report and
per-module compiler results are included in the supplement.

The upper certificate uses 13 landmark trees. For each landmark $r$ an
integer rank $q_r(v)$ and a parent are supplied, with $q_r(v)\leq b_r$ for
every $v$, and
\[
v\ne r\ \Longrightarrow\
\bigl(vp_r(v)\in E(G)\ \text{and}\ q_r(p_r(v))+1\leq q_r(v)\bigr).
\]
The first two conditions give a walk from $v$ to $r$ of length at most
$q_r(v)$ by induction on the rank. The landmarks and bounds are
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrrrrrrrrr}
r&0&412&405&73&384&388&52&120&47&618&35&39&194\\
b_r&15&19&20&20&20&19&20&19&20&20&20&20&19
\end{array}
\]
The 20 supplied label actions are checked to preserve adjacency and to have
the supplied inverses. For every $u$, a landmark $r$ and action $g$ satisfy
\[
q_r(g^{-1}u)+b_r\leq21.
\]
The paths $u\to gr\to v$ therefore have length at most 21 for every $v$.
This construction checks 62,712 tree entries, rather than formalizing a BFS
implementation. The same kernel checks also verify the potential inequalities
on every edge and the endpoint values 0 and 21. The generic soundness proof is
\texttt{landmark\_diameter\_certificate} in \texttt{lean/GraphCertificate.lean}.

The formal theorem concerns the explicit relation \texttt{Fixed.Adj} on
\texttt{Fin 4824}. An independent decoder reads the literal Lean tables and
checks exact equality with every CSV neighbor, the label actions on the census,
and the witness potential. The identification of that graph with the geometric
flip graph relies on Sections 3--5 and the independently executed enumeration
and flip construction. Convex geometry and enumeration completeness are not
formalized in Lean. The radius, list of seven diametral pairs and their orbit
sizes remain exact computational results checked outside Lean.

\section{Certificates, Reproducibility and Limitations}
Run \texttt{python3 verify/independent\_check.py} from the project root. It
rebuilds exact geometry, independently re-enumerates the full census, rebuilds
all flips, checks the internal-triangle graph, and recomputes all distances.
It prints \texttt{VERIFIED} only after every check succeeds. Do not disable
Python assertions with \texttt{-O} or \texttt{PYTHONOPTIMIZE}.
The mathematical core uses only the Python standard library. Seven regression
and negative tests also pass. TOPCOM is optional. The Williams comparison additionally requires NumPy and
SciPy; ReportLab is needed only to regenerate the accompanying reports.
The primary checker does not depend on these packages.

The smaller cases are regression checks, not evidence for a formula beyond
the stated finite computations.

Every triangulation uses sorted vertex labels and sorted maximal simplices;
the full census is lexicographically sorted. Its index defines the graph ID.
Individual hashes use compact ASCII JSON without a trailing newline.
The distance matrix is hashed as row-major unsigned bytes, with distances
$0,\ldots,21$ and 255 reserved for unreachable pairs. Its SHA-256 is
\begin{center}\small\texttt{c384c92a7ef367f11d8f08a27f5e7c6bbcc0d7b98c674c9689dbc451fee22a13}.\end{center}
File hashes are integrity aids; the checker recomputes the mathematical facts.
The trusted basis consists of the geometric and algorithmic arguments above,
the inspected implementations, Python integer semantics, and the execution
platform. The finite graph diameter has the additional kernel-checked Lean
certificate above; the geometric identification retains the stated trust boundary.
